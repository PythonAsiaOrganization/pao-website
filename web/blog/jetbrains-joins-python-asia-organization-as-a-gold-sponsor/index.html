JetBrains Joins Python Asia Organization as a Gold Sponsor

We’re welcoming JetBrains as a Gold Sponsor of Python Asia Organization (PAO).

JetBrains builds professional tools for developers and teams around the world. In the Python community, many people know the company through PyCharm, its professional Python IDE for web development, data, machine learning, and other Python workflows.

That gives JetBrains a clear connection to the ecosystem PAO works in. But the reason this sponsorship matters to us goes beyond developer tools.

It helps support the people and communities doing the work between conferences: organizing meetups, sharing lessons, introducing communities to one another, and finding better ways to collaborate across borders.

JetBrains, PyCharm, and the Python Community

PyCharm has been part of the Python developer landscape for years.

Developers use it across web development, data, machine learning, and day-to-day Python work. It brings coding, debugging, testing, project navigation, and other development tools into one environment, whether someone is working on a small project or maintaining something much larger.

JetBrains’ connection to Python also extends beyond the product itself.

The company has supported developer and open-source communities, including organizations within the Python ecosystem. That matters to PAO because regional community building depends on the same kind of long-term thinking.

Events are important. So is everything that happens before and after them.

Connecting Python Communities Across Asia

Python Asia Organization works to bring Python communities across East and Southeast Asia closer together.

Those communities do not all operate under the same conditions.

Some have established conferences, strong volunteer networks, and long-standing sponsor relationships. Others are smaller, newer, or working with fewer resources. Organizers face different challenges depending on where they are, but many of the problems are familiar: finding speakers, keeping volunteers engaged, funding events, sharing knowledge, and reaching beyond their local networks.

There is a lot communities can learn from one another.

PAO tries to make those exchanges easier.

That might mean bringing organizers into the same conversation, sharing an opportunity across countries, helping one community learn from another, or creating programs where people can hear directly from the teams doing the work.

Sometimes it becomes a formal collaboration.

Sometimes it is simply an introduction that would not have happened otherwise.

Both matter.

The Work That Happens Between Conferences

Conferences are often the most visible part of a Python community.

People travel. Talks happen. Organizers finally meet people they may have known online for years. A few days can create conversations and relationships that would have taken months to form remotely.

Then everyone goes home.

The work does not stop.

Someone starts preparing the next meetup. Another organizer is looking for a speaker. A community is trying to improve its sponsorship process. Someone else is working out how to make an event more accessible, recruit more volunteers, or avoid a mistake another group has already learned from.

This is where regional relationships become useful.

An idea tested in one country can save another community weeks of work. An introduction can turn into a speaker exchange, a partnership, or a future event. A conversation that starts casually can become something much bigger later.

A large part of PAO’s work happens in these smaller moments.

Python Across Asia gives communities a place to talk about what they are building, what they have learned, and the people behind the work.

The PythonAsia conference creates a different kind of opportunity: a shared regional gathering where those relationships can happen face to face.

They are different programs, but they come from the same idea.

Communities are stronger when they do not have to work in isolation.

Why This Sponsorship Matters

A useful sponsorship should do more than put a company logo on a website.

For PAO, sponsorship gives us more room to keep doing the work that is harder to see from the outside: connecting organizers, running regional programs, maintaining partnerships, sharing opportunities, and helping communities reach beyond their usual circles.

The results are not always easy to measure in a single number.

Sometimes two organizers meet and decide to work together.

Sometimes a community finds a speaker through another country’s network.

Sometimes someone discovers a conference outside their own region, attends it, volunteers, speaks, or brings an idea home.

None of those things looks especially dramatic on its own.

But repeated often enough, they change how communities relate to one another.

That is how separate local groups begin to feel like part of something regional.

What JetBrains’ Gold Sponsorship Supports

PAO is still building.

There are more communities we want to reach, more organizers we want to connect, and more opportunities to create for people working across the region.

JetBrains’ Gold Sponsorship helps give us the capacity to keep that work moving through PAO’s programs, events, partnerships, and community initiatives.

For us, the goal is not simply to run more activities.

It is to make regional collaboration easier to access and easier to sustain.

We want organizers to know who they can turn to outside their own country. We want communities to be able to share what they have learned instead of solving the same problems from scratch. And we want more people to discover the work happening across Python communities in Asia.

That takes time.

It also takes partners willing to support the work behind the scenes.

We’re glad to welcome JetBrains as part of it.
